# Bond Paper

## What is Bond Paper?
Bond paper is a **high‑quality, durable writing and printing paper** originally developed for **official documents** such as contracts and legal paperwork. Today, it is commonly used for **letters, reports, coursework, and printed design work**.

In Product Design, bond paper is used for **written communication, annotations, and high‑quality printed pages**, rather than sketching or modelling.

---

## Structure and Composition
Bond paper is made from:

- **High‑quality wood pulp fibres**
- Often a higher cotton or fibre content than standard copier paper
- Fillers to improve strength and surface finish

Key characteristics:
- **Opaque**
- Smooth surface
- Stronger and more durable than standard copier paper
- Available in different weights (e.g. 90–120 gsm)

Its structure allows it to withstand **handling, folding, and writing without tearing easily**.

---

## Manufacturing Process
1. Wood pulp is processed and refined for strength
2. Fibres are mixed with fillers and additives
3. The pulp slurry is spread onto a moving mesh
4. Water is removed through pressing and drying
5. The paper is calendered to produce a smooth surface
6. Sheets are cut to standard sizes and packaged

Bond paper is manufactured to prioritise **strength and print clarity**.

## Advantages
- Strong and durable
- High‑quality appearance
- Good for writing and printing
- Resistant to tearing
- Suitable for formal and professional work
- Widely recyclable

---

## Disadvantages
- More expensive than copier paper
- Not suitable for tracing
- Less suitable for rapid sketching
- Limited use in early design stages
- Heavier paper increases material usage

---

## Typical Uses
Bond paper is commonly used for:

- **Formal documents**
- **Coursework write‑ups**
- **Printed research pages**
- **Design annotations**
- **High‑quality printed CAD drawings**

It is often chosen when a **professional finish** is required.

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Made from renewable wood pulp
- Long lifespan compared to thin papers
- Often available with recycled content
- Recyclable

### Disadvantages
- Uses more material than copier paper
- Environmental impact depends on pulp source
- Single‑use in many educational contexts

### Improvements
- FSC‑certified bond paper
- Increased recycled fibre content
- Reduced paper use through digital submission

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Very low risk material
- Minor risk of paper cuts
- Safe for classroom and office use
- No PPE required

## Suitability for Product Design
Bond paper is suitable when:
- Producing written coursework
- Printing high‑quality documents
- Annotating designs neatly
- Presenting research clearly

Bond paper is not suitable when:
- Sketching ideas
- Tracing or overlaying drawings
- Making models or prototypes
- Producing informal concept work

## Overall Summary
Bond paper is a **strong, high‑quality, opaque paper** designed for **professional writing and printing**. In A Level Product Design, it is most commonly used for **coursework write‑ups, printed research, annotations, and high‑quality CAD outputs**, rather than sketching or modelling. Although more expensive than standard copier paper, its **durability and clean finish** make it suitable for formal presentation and communication. Bond paper should be evaluated in terms of its **material properties, sustainability, and role within the research and documentation stages of the design process**.
