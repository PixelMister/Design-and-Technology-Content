# Grid 6: Final Design Solution

## Final Design Solution
- Maximum marks: 9
- Indicative portfolio space: approximately 3–5 A3 sides

## What are you trying to achieve?

You must present a final design that satisfies your specification and contains enough technical information for a third party to interpret how the product should be manufactured.

The delivery guide emphasises comprehensive working drawings, material quantities, costs, manufacturing details and methods of reducing waste.

## 6.1 Present a complete final design
Include a clear overview of the product.

**Show:**
-	Overall form.
-	Main features.
-	Intended operation.
-	Relevant dimensions.
-	Material choices.
-	Surface finishes.
-	Component relationships.
-	Important ergonomic features.

Where appropriate, show the product in more than one position.

!!! example "How could this be demonstrated?"
    A folding product may need both operating and storage configurations.

## 6.2 Produce accurate working drawings
Your working drawings should include the information required to manufacture the prototype.

**Consider:**
-	Front elevation.
-	Side elevation.
-	Plan.
-	Isometric view.
-	Sectional views.
-	Detail views.
-	Component drawings.
-	Exploded assemblies.

Include relevant dimensions and tolerances.

**Ensure:**
-	Dimensions are clearly labelled.
-	Views are consistent.
-	The scale is appropriate.
-	Materials are identified.
-	Construction details are visible.
-	Important features are not left unexplained.

## 6.3 Produce detailed component drawings

Complex products may require separate drawings for individual components.

**For each component, consider:**
-	Component name or reference.
-	Material.
-	Quantity.
-	Length, width and thickness.
-	Hole positions.
-	Angles or radii.
-	Tolerances.
-	Surface finish.
-	Special machining requirements.

These details should reduce ambiguity during manufacture.

## 6.4 Produce a cutting list or bill of materials
Create a complete list of the materials and components required.

| Component | Material | Quantity | Dimensions | Additional Information |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Main Support** | Selected structural material | As required | From working drawing | Include finish |
| **Connecting Member** | Selected material | As required | From component drawing | Include joining details |
| **Pivot Fitting** | Suitable standard component | As required | Supplier specification | Check required performance |
| **Fasteners** | Appropriate specification | As required | Supplier specification | Check compatibility |

Replace these placeholders with the exact details for your project.

## 6.5 Include material and component justification

Explain why your final material choices are appropriate.

**Consider:**
-	Relevant properties.
-	Structural demands.
-	Weight.
-	Durability.
-	Finish.
-	Cost.
-	Manufacturing feasibility.
-	Sustainability.
-	User requirements.

You should be able to justify the choice of each significant material or component.

## 6.6 Consider sustainability in your final design

Explain how environmental decisions have influenced your design.

**These might involve:**
-	Reducing material waste.
-	Using appropriate stock sizes.
-	Selecting responsibly sourced materials.
-	Designing for repair.
-	Avoiding unnecessary components.
-	Extending product life.
-	Choosing suitable finishes.
-	Considering end-of-life disposal.

The important point is to explain the implications of your choices.

## 6.7 Calculate material quantities and costs
You should use appropriate calculations to determine how much material is required.

For example, where relevant:
Material cost = Quantity required × Unit cost

Total estimated material cost = Sum of individual material and component costs

**You may need to consider:**
-	Stock sizes.
-	Cutting allowances.
-	Wastage.
-	Bought-in components.
-	Fasteners.
-	Finishing materials.
-	Consumables.

Your estimates should use actual supplier information or clearly identified assumptions.

## 6.8 Plan to reduce wastage
Consider how components could be arranged when cutting materials.
You might produce a cutting layout showing how parts can be positioned on a sheet or length of stock.

**Explain:**
-	Why the arrangement is efficient.
-	What waste remains.
-	Whether offcuts could be reused.
-	Whether alternative stock sizes would reduce waste.
-	Whether changing component dimensions could improve material efficiency without compromising function.

## 6.9 Produce a detailed production plan
Your production plan should identify the correct sequence of manufacturing operations.

**Include:**
-	Manufacturing stage.
-	Component.
-	Process.
-	Materials.
-	Tools and equipment.
-	Quality control checks.
-	Safety requirements.
-	Estimated time or target completion.
-	Sustainability considerations where relevant.

### Production Plan Example
| Stage | Operation | Tools/Equipment | Quality Check | Safety Consideration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Prepare stock material | Appropriate measuring and cutting tools | Confirm stock dimensions | Follow relevant workshop procedures |
| **2** | Mark out components | Measuring and marking equipment | Check dimensions against drawing | Secure workpiece appropriately |
| **3** | Manufacture components | Selected machinery and hand tools | Check size, geometry and surface quality | Use approved methods and supervision |
| **4** | Test-fit components | Suitable assembly tools | Check alignment and fit | Avoid unsafe handling |
| **5** | Complete assembly | Selected joining equipment | Check joint quality and overall geometry | Follow relevant safety controls |
| **6** | Apply finish | Suitable finishing equipment | Inspect consistency and quality | Follow product and workshop guidance |

Your actual production plan must be specific to your project.


## 6.10 Identify quality control points
Do not leave quality checking until the final assembly.

Identify which dimensions or features are particularly important.

**For example:**
-	Hole centre positions.
-	Joint dimensions.
-	Component alignment.
-	Clearances.
-	Angles.
-	Overall dimensions.
-	Surface condition.

Describe how you will check these features during manufacture.

## 6.11 Consider assembly and maintenance

**Think about:**
-	How components will fit together.
-	Whether assembly is practical.
-	Whether fasteners can be accessed.
-	Whether damaged parts can be replaced.
-	Whether the product can be cleaned.
-	Whether moving components require maintenance.

These considerations may influence your final design.

**Common weaknesses to avoid**
-	Including only an attractive final render.
-	Omitting important dimensions.
-	Using inconsistent dimensions across drawings.
-	Failing to provide component details.
-	Producing an incomplete cutting list.
-	Ignoring costs or material quantities.
-	Including a production plan that is too vague to follow.
-	Failing to consider waste reduction.
