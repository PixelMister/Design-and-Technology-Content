# Grid 8: Communication of Design Ideas

## Communication of Design Ideas
- Maximum marks: 6
- Assessment applies throughout your design portfolio

**What are you trying to achieve?**

You must demonstrate an accomplished and appropriate use of traditional graphical techniques, computer-aided design and written communication.

This assessment area is concerned with how effectively you communicate your design thinking.

## 8.1 Select techniques for a clear purpose
Different communication methods are useful for different tasks.
Purpose	Suitable communication technique

| Purpose | Appropriate Method |
|---|---|
| **Explore a new idea** | Thumbnail sketches and freehand drawing |
| **Communicate overall form** | Isometric or perspective views |
| **Communicate dimensions** | Orthographic working drawings |
| **Show assembly** | Exploded or sectional drawings |
| **Investigate proportions** | Physical models or CAD |
| **Investigate component fit** | Detailed CAD models |
| **Explain mechanisms** | Movement diagrams or sequences |
| **Record manufacture** | Annotated photographs |
| **Compare alternatives** | Tables and analytical annotations |
| **Present test results** | Measurements, tables and graphs |

Select the method that communicates the information most effectively.

## 8.2 Demonstrate traditional drawing skills
**Use appropriate graphical techniques such as:**
-	Freehand sketching.
-	Isometric drawing.
-	Perspective drawing.
-	Orthographic projection.
-	Exploded diagrams.
-	Sectional views.
-	Detailed component drawings.
-	Tonal rendering.

Accuracy and clarity are important.

## 8.3 Demonstrate CAD skills
Use CAD to support relevant stages of the design process.

**You might include:**
-	Development models.
-	Parametric component models.
-	Assembly models.
-	Dimensioned drawings.
-	Exploded assemblies.
-	Rendered representations.
-	Mechanism investigations.
-	Manufacturing preparation.

Explain what the CAD work contributes to your design.

## 8.4 Use precise written annotations
Your annotations should communicate reasoning, not simply label features.

!!! example "Annotations"

    === "Weak"
        Aluminium frame.

    === "Stronger"
        The aluminium frame reduces mass while providing the necessary structural arrangement. The selected section will be checked through modelling and testing to confirm that its stiffness is suitable for the intended loading.

## 8.5 Structure your pages carefully
**A well-organised page should have:**
-	A clear title.
-	A specific purpose.
-	Suitable visual evidence.
-	Technical annotations.
-	A conclusion or decision.
-	A link to the next stage where appropriate.

**Avoid:**
-	Overcrowded pages.
-	Excessive decorative graphics.
-	Small, unreadable text.
-	Repeated images.
-	Unexplained CAD renders.
-	Long paragraphs that repeat what the drawings already show.

## 8.6 Check technical accuracy
Use correct terminology.

**Distinguish between:**
-	Strength and stiffness.
-	Mass and weight.
-	Accuracy and precision.
-	Testing and evaluation.
-	Thermoplastics and thermosetting polymers.
-	Sustainability and recyclability.

Make sure your drawings, dimensions and written information do not contradict one another.
