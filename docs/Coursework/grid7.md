# Grid 7: Review of Development and Final Design

## Review of Development and Final Design
- *Maximum marks: 12*
- *Suggested approach: ongoing evaluation plus a focused formal review*

**What are you trying to achieve?**

You must demonstrate your ability to analyse and evaluate your own design development and the work of other designers.

You should make balanced judgements supported by evidence.

This assessment area is not limited to a single final page. 

Evidence should be included throughout the design and development process.

## 7.1 Understand what critical review means
A critical review is more than identifying strengths and weaknesses.

**You should:**
1.	Identify the design feature or decision.
2.	Explain its intended purpose.
3.	Examine the evidence available.
4.	Consider advantages and disadvantages.
5.	Compare alternatives where appropriate.
6.	Evaluate how successfully the feature meets the requirements.
7.	Reach a supported conclusion.

## 7.2 Review important design changes
Select the most significant decisions made during development.

**For each, consider:**
-	What was the original approach?
-	Why was it selected?
-	What problem or limitation was identified?
-	What alternatives were investigated?
-	What evidence was collected?
-	Why was the revised approach selected?
-	How did the change affect the final design?

!!! example "Design Change Review"
    The initial design used a fixed support arrangement, which provided a simple construction method but did not address the client's requirement for compact storage. A folding support was subsequently investigated. Although this increased the complexity of the mechanism, modelling demonstrated that the product could occupy less space when stored. Further work was required to ensure that the moving connection maintained adequate stability.

This demonstrates both benefits and limitations.

## 7.3 Analyse the work of other designers

You should also demonstrate an ability to examine other designs and prototypes critically.

**Select relevant examples and investigate:**
-	Function.
-	Form and aesthetics.
-	Materials.
-	Manufacturing techniques.
-	Construction.
-	Ergonomics.
-	Sustainability.
-	User requirements.

Explain how the different features contribute to the overall product.

!!! warning "Avoid"
    Avoid analysing products in isolation from your own project.
    You should demonstrate how these investigations influence your design decisions.

## 7.4 Compare your design against the specification
Use your specification to identify how effectively the final design addresses the original requirements.

| Specification Requirement | Design Response | Evidence | Judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Compact Storage** | Folding arrangement | CAD model and mock-up | Appears promising; final dimensions need confirmation |
| **Stability** | Reinforced support | Joint model and testing | Improvement demonstrated in development test |
| **Suitable Appearance** | Selected form and finish | Client feedback | Client preference is supported |
| **Appropriate Materials** | Selected material combination | Material research | Technically appropriate, subject to final manufacture |

Your review should identify unresolved issues as well as successful decisions.

## 7.5 Evaluate compromises
Design decisions often involve competing requirements.
For example:
-	Strength versus mass.
-	Complexity versus functionality.
-	Appearance versus ease of manufacture.
-	Cost versus durability.
-	Adjustability versus simplicity.

Explain how you have balanced these factors.

!!! warning "Do Not!"
    Do not claim that every decision is perfect.

A balanced evaluation demonstrates that you understand the limitations as well as the advantages of your chosen approach.

## 7.6 Consider the effectiveness of your iterative process
Reflect on the development process itself.

**Ask:**
-	Which experiments were most useful?
-	Which models revealed important problems?
-	Did client feedback influence significant decisions?
-	Were some investigations unnecessary?
-	Did any research lead you to reconsider your approach?
-	What would you investigate further if time allowed?

## 7.7 Produce a formal review of your final design

**Your review should include:**
-	A summary of major refinements.
-	Evidence supporting important decisions.
-	Strengths of the final design.
-	Remaining weaknesses or uncertainties.
-	Comparison against the specification.
-	Client feedback.
-	Justification for proceeding to manufacture.

**Common weaknesses to avoid**
-	Writing only positive comments.
-	Repeating descriptions of design features.
-	Using unsupported judgements.
-	Ignoring the influence of other designers.
-	Failing to explain how feedback informed development.
-	Leaving all evaluation until the end.
-	Claiming that the product meets every requirement before it has been tested.
