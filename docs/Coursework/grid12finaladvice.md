# Think and Work Like a Designer

Your NEA should communicate a complete process of design thinking.

You should begin with a genuine problem, not a predetermined product. Your investigation should identify the needs of the client and inform a meaningful specification.

Your design work should explore possibilities and demonstrate creativity supported by technical understanding.

Your development should show experimentation, testing and improvements. It is important to show when an idea did not work, provided that you explain what you learnt and how your design changed.

Your final design should communicate the information necessary for manufacture, and your prototype should demonstrate advanced-level practical skills, appropriate accuracy and quality.

Finally, your evaluation should establish how successfully the product performs using real evidence rather than unsupported personal judgements.

The strongest NEA portfolios do not simply show the journey from an idea to a finished product. They explain and justify the decisions made throughout that journey.

**At every stage, return to the same five questions:**
1. What did I do? 
2. Why did I do it? 
3. What did I discover? 
4. How did it influence my design? 
5. What should happen next?

If you can answer these questions clearly and support your responses with relevant evidence, you will be developing a portfolio that demonstrates the research, design, technical and practical understanding expected at A-Level.
