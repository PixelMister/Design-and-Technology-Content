# Total Quality Management (TQM) and ISO 9000

## What Is Total Quality Management (TQM)?
Total Quality Management (TQM) is a **company‑wide approach to quality** that focuses on **continuous improvement** and **customer satisfaction**. Unlike quality control, which checks products, TQM aims to **embed quality into every stage of the organisation**.

In Product Design, TQM is applied to **quality assurance procedures** to ensure that **every employee**, at **every stage of the production process**, takes responsibility for quality.

---

## Key Principles of Total Quality Management

TQM is based on several core principles:

### 1. Customer Focus
- The customer defines quality
- Products must meet or exceed customer expectations
- Customer satisfaction is the ultimate measure of success

✅ Links directly to **user‑centred design (UCD)**.

---

### 2. Continuous Improvement
- Quality is never “finished”
- Processes are constantly reviewed and improved
- Problems are opportunities to improve systems

✅ Often referred to as **Kaizen** (continuous improvement).

---

### 3. Everyone Is Responsible for Quality
- Quality is not just the responsibility of inspectors or managers
- All employees contribute to quality outcomes

✅ Quality becomes part of workplace culture.

---

### 4. Process‑Centred Approach
- Focus on improving **processes**, not blaming individuals
- Prevent errors rather than fixing them later

✅ Strong link to **quality assurance**, not quality control.

---

### 5. Data‑Driven Decision Making
- Decisions based on evidence and data
- Use of inspection data, testing results, and feedback

✅ Reduces guesswork and inconsistency.

---

## TQM Applied to Quality Assurance Procedures

TQM supports quality assurance by ensuring quality is considered at **every stage of the product lifecycle**.

---

## 1. Design and Development Stage
Under TQM:
- Designers consider quality from the start
- Clear design specifications are created
- Tolerances are realistic and achievable
- Prototyping and testing are used early
- Design for Manufacture (DfM) and Design for Assembly (DfA) are applied

✅ Problems are designed out before manufacture begins.

---

## 2. Manufacturing Stage
TQM influences manufacturing by:
- Using standardised processes
- Ensuring machines are calibrated
- Training staff properly
- Monitoring processes continuously
- Encouraging workers to identify defects early

✅ Quality is built into the process, not inspected in at the end.

---

## 3. Inspection and Testing
In TQM:
- Inspection supports improvement, not blame
- Data from inspection is used to improve processes
- Computer‑aided testing (CAT) provides accurate feedback

✅ Testing feeds back into continuous improvement.

---

## 4. End‑Use Performance and Customer Satisfaction
TQM extends beyond the factory:
- Customer feedback is collected
- Complaints and returns are analysed
- Product performance in real use is monitored
- Improvements are made to future designs

✅ Customer satisfaction is central to TQM.

---

## Impact of TQM on Employees

TQM has a **significant impact on employees at every stage of production**.

---

### Increased Responsibility
- Employees take ownership of quality
- Workers are encouraged to stop production if faults are found

✅ Improves accountability.

---

### Training and Skill Development
- Employees receive ongoing training
- Workers understand how their role affects quality

✅ Leads to higher skill levels.

---

### Improved Motivation and Morale
- Employees feel valued
- Workers contribute ideas for improvement
- Less blame culture

✅ Can improve job satisfaction.

---

### Teamwork and Communication
- Departments work together
- Designers, manufacturers, and quality teams collaborate

✅ Reduces errors caused by poor communication.

---

### Possible Disadvantages for Employees
- Increased pressure to meet standards
- Resistance to change
- Requires cultural change within organisations

❌ TQM must be managed carefully to avoid stress.

---

## Advantages of Total Quality Management
- Higher and more consistent quality
- Fewer defects and less waste
- Improved customer satisfaction
- Reduced long‑term costs
- Strong brand reputation
- Better employee involvement
- Continuous improvement culture

---

## Disadvantages of Total Quality Management
- Time‑consuming to implement
- Requires commitment from all staff
- Initial training costs
- Cultural resistance to change
- Not effective if poorly managed

## ISO 9000

## What Is ISO 9000?
ISO 9000 is a **family of international quality management standards** that provides a **framework for quality assurance systems**. The most commonly referenced standard is **ISO 9001**.

ISO standards do not guarantee perfect products, but they ensure that **processes are consistent and controlled**.

---

## Purpose of ISO 9000
ISO 9000 aims to:
- Standardise quality management systems
- Ensure consistency in production
- Improve customer confidence
- Support continuous improvement
- Provide external verification of quality systems

✅ ISO certification demonstrates commitment to quality.

---

## Key Features of ISO 9000
- Documented procedures
- Clear responsibilities
- Training requirements
- Regular audits
- Continuous improvement
- Traceability of processes

✅ Focuses on **how quality is managed**, not just outcomes.

---

## ISO 9000 and Quality Assurance
ISO 9000 supports QA by:
- Requiring documented quality procedures
- Ensuring quality is considered from design to delivery
- Monitoring performance and customer satisfaction
- Encouraging corrective and preventive actions

✅ ISO 9000 complements TQM principles.

---

## TQM vs ISO 9000

| Aspect | Total Quality Management | ISO 9000 |
|------|--------------------------|----------|
| Nature | Philosophy and culture | Formal standard |
| Focus | Continuous improvement | Consistent processes |
| Employee involvement | Very high | Structured |
| Certification | No | Yes |
| Flexibility | High | More rigid |

✅ Many companies use **both together**.

## Overall Summary
Total Quality Management (TQM) is a **holistic approach to quality** that embeds **continuous improvement and customer satisfaction** into every stage of the production process. When applied to quality assurance procedures, TQM ensures that **all employees**, from designers to factory workers, share responsibility for maintaining high standards. This leads to improved quality, reduced waste, and higher employee engagement. **ISO 9000** supports this approach by providing an **international framework for consistent quality management**, ensuring processes are documented, monitored, and improved. In **A Level Product Design**, understanding TQM and ISO 9000 is essential for explaining how organisations achieve **reliable quality, motivated employees, and satisfied customers** in modern manufacturing.
