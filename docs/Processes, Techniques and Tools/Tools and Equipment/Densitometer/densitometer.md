# Densitometer

## What Is a Densitometer?
A densitometer is a **specialist quality‑control tool used in printing** to measure the **density (darkness) of ink on a printed surface**. It checks how much light is absorbed by the ink, helping printers ensure **accurate colour, consistency, and print quality**.

In A Level Product Design, densitometers are mainly linked to **commercial printing processes** such as:
- Offset lithography
- Flexography
- Gravure
- Screen printing

---

## What Does a Densitometer Measure?
A densitometer measures:
- **Ink density** (how dark or light the print is)
- **Consistency of ink coverage**
- **Colour strength** (especially CMYK colours)

It does **not** measure size or shape — it measures **print quality**.

---

## How a Densitometer Works
1. The densitometer shines a **controlled light** onto the printed surface
2. The ink absorbs some of the light
3. The remaining light is reflected back into the sensor
4. The device calculates how much light was absorbed
5. A **density value** is displayed on the screen

✅ **Darker ink = higher density value**  
✅ **Lighter ink = lower density value**

---

## Types of Densitometer

### Reflection Densitometer (Most Common)
- Measures light reflected from the print
- Used on:
  - Paper
  - Card
  - Packaging

✅ This is the type most relevant to **A Level Product Design**.

---

### Transmission Densitometer
- Measures light passing through a material
- Used mainly for:
  - Film
  - Transparencies

Less common at A Level.

---

## CMYK and Densitometers
Densitometers are used to control **CMYK colour printing**:

- **C** – Cyan  
- **M** – Magenta  
- **Y** – Yellow  
- **K** – Black  

Each colour has an **ideal density range**.  
The densitometer checks that each colour layer is printed correctly.

---

## Why Densitometers Are Used in Printing
Densitometers are used to:
- Ensure consistent print quality
- Prevent colour variation between print runs
- Reduce waste
- Detect printing faults early
- Maintain professional standards

Without densitometers, prints may appear:
- Too dark
- Too pale
- Inconsistent
- Poor quality

---

## Where Densitometers Are Used
Densitometers are used in:

- **Offset lithography**
- **Flexography**
- **Gravure printing**
- **Screen printing**
- **Packaging production**
- **Commercial print shops**

They are part of **industrial printing quality control**.

## Densitometer vs Spectrophotometer
| Feature | Densitometer | Spectrophotometer |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| Measures | Ink density | Colour values |
| Accuracy | High | Very high |
| Complexity | Simple | Complex |
| Typical Use | Production control | Colour matching |
| A Level focus | ✅ Yes | Limited |

At A Level, you only need to understand **densitometers**.

---

## Advantages of Using a Densitometer
- Improves colour accuracy
- Ensures consistency
- Reduces waste
- Saves time
- Improves professional finish
- Essential for mass production

---

## Disadvantages of Using a Densitometer
- Expensive equipment
- Requires trained operators
- Industrial use only
- Does not measure colour hue (only density)

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Safe handheld device
- Used away from moving machinery
- Minimal risk
- Used as part of controlled industrial printing environments

No major hazards, but still part of **professional print control systems**.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
A densitometer is suitable when:
- Explaining **quality control in printing**
- Comparing printing processes
- Justifying professional manufacturing methods
- Discussing batch and mass production
- Linking accuracy to sustainability (reduced waste)

It is **not used in school workshops**, but must be understood theoretically.

## Overall Summary
A densitometer is a **specialist printing quality‑control tool** used to measure **ink density on printed materials**, ensuring **consistent colour strength and professional print quality**. By measuring how much light ink absorbs, it helps printers control **CMYK colour balance**, reduce waste, and maintain standards in **commercial printing processes** such as offset lithography and flexography. Although densitometers are **industrial tools and not used in school workshops**, understanding their role is essential in **A Level Product Design** when evaluating printing processes, mass production, sustainability, and quality assurance.
