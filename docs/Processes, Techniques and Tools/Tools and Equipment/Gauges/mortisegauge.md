# Mortise Gauge

## What Is a Mortise Gauge?
A mortise gauge is a **specialist woodworking marking out tool** used to mark **two parallel lines at the same time**, specifically for **mortise and tenon joints**. It ensures that the **mortise (slot) and tenon (tongue) match perfectly**, giving a strong and accurate joint.

In A Level Product Design, the mortise gauge is essential for **precision joint marking** and **traditional woodworking techniques**.

---

## Main Parts of a Mortise Gauge
A mortise gauge is similar to a marking gauge but has additional features.

### Stock (Fence)
- Flat block that rests against the face edge of the timber

**Purpose:**
- Keeps the gauge aligned
- Ensures lines are parallel to the reference edge

---

### Beam (Stem)
- Adjustable shaft that passes through the stock

**Purpose:**
- Sets the distance from the edge
- Controls the position of the joint lines

---

### Two Spurs / Pins
- Two sharp metal points
- One fixed, one adjustable

**Purpose:**
- Mark the **width of the mortise**
- Ensure the tenon matches exactly

The spacing between the pins is set to the **width of the chisel** used for the mortise.

---

## How a Mortise Gauge Is Used
1. Select the correct mortise chisel
2. Adjust the two pins to match the width of the chisel
3. Set the beam to the correct distance from the edge
4. Lock the settings securely
5. Hold the stock firmly against the face edge
6. Pull the gauge along the timber
7. Two **parallel, accurate lines** are scored

The same settings are used to mark **both the mortise and the tenon**.

---

## Why a Mortise Gauge Is Important
Without a mortise gauge:
- Mortises and tenons may not align
- Joints may be loose or too tight
- Strength and appearance are reduced

The mortise gauge ensures:
- Accuracy
- Consistency
- Strong, professional‑quality joints

---

## Mortise Gauge vs Marking Gauge
| Feature | Marking Gauge | Mortise Gauge |
|------|---------------|---------------|
| Number of pins | One | Two |
| Marks | Single line | Two parallel lines |
| Main use | General marking | Mortise & tenon joints |
| Accuracy for joints | Medium | Very high |

A mortise gauge is a **specialist version** of a marking gauge.

---

## Typical Uses of a Mortise Gauge
A mortise gauge is used for:

- **Mortise and tenon joints**
- **Frame construction**
- **Furniture making**
- **Door frames**
- **Tables and chairs**
- **Cabinet making**

It is rarely used for general marking out.

---

## Best Practice When Using a Mortise Gauge
- Always mark from the **face side or face edge**
- Set the pins using the **actual chisel**, not a ruler
- Apply light, even pressure
- Pull the gauge towards you
- Keep pins sharp
- Clearly mark the waste area
- Do not adjust settings between marking mortise and tenon

Consistency is critical for joint accuracy.

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Pins are sharp – handle with care
- Keep fingers clear of the pins
- Store with pins protected
- Do not force the tool
- Ensure timber is supported or clamped

Correct handling reduces the risk of cuts or slips.

---

## Mortise Gauge Compared to Other Marking Tools
| Tool | Best Use | Accuracy |
|----|--------|----------|
| Pencil | Rough marking | Medium |
| Marking Gauge | Parallel lines | Very High |
| Cutting Gauge | Cross‑grain work | Extremely High |
| Mortise Gauge | Mortise & tenon joints | Extremely High |

The mortise gauge is the **most accurate tool for joint width marking**.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
The mortise gauge is suitable when:
- Traditional wood joints are required
- High accuracy is essential
- Strength and fit are critical
- Professional‑quality joinery is expected

It is less suitable when:
- Rough layout work is required
- Speed is more important than precision
- Joints are not being used

## Overall Summary
A mortise gauge is a **specialist marking out tool used in woodworking** to mark **two parallel lines simultaneously**, ensuring accurate and matching **mortise and tenon joints**. With its **adjustable twin pins**, it allows the joint width to be set precisely to the mortise chisel, guaranteeing strength and consistency. Compared to a standard marking gauge, the mortise gauge offers **greater precision for joint work** and is essential in **furniture making and traditional joinery**. In A Level Product Design, the mortise gauge should be understood as a **precision marking tool** that underpins **accurate joint construction, high build quality, and safe woodworking practice**.
