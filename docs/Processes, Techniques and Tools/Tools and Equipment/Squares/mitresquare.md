# Mitre Square

## What Is a Mitre Square?
A mitre square is a **specialist marking out and checking tool** used to **mark and check accurate 45° angles**, most commonly in **woodworking**. It is specifically designed for **mitre joints**, where two pieces meet at 45° to form a 90° corner.

In A Level Product Design, the mitre square is used to ensure **accurate angled joints**, particularly in **frames, mouldings, and decorative woodwork**.

---

## Main Parts of a Mitre Square
A mitre square has a similar construction to a try square, but the blade is fixed at **45° instead of 90°**.

### Stock (Handle)
- Thicker part of the tool
- Usually made from wood, plastic, or metal

**Purpose:**
- Rests against the face edge of the material
- Acts as the reference surface

---

### Blade
- Thin metal blade fixed at **45°** to the stock

**Purpose:**
- Used for marking angled lines
- Used for checking mitre cuts

The blade must be **accurately set** to ensure clean mitre joints.

---

## How a Mitre Square Is Used
1. Place the **stock firmly against the face edge** of the timber
2. Hold the tool flat on the surface
3. Use a pencil or marking knife to draw along the blade
4. Remove the mitre square carefully
5. Cut along the marked line
6. Use the mitre square again to **check the cut for accuracy**

This ensures both pieces of the joint meet cleanly.

---

## What a Mitre Square Is Used For
A mitre square is used to:

- Mark **45° angles**
- Check mitre cuts
- Lay out **mitre joints**
- Ensure angled components fit accurately
- Assist with frame and moulding construction

It is mainly used in **woodworking**, but can be used on plastics and soft materials.

---

## Typical Mitre Joints
Mitre squares are commonly used for:

- **Picture frames**
- **Door and window frames**
- **Skirting boards**
- **Architraves**
- **Decorative frames**
- **Box corners**

Mitre joints are chosen for their **neat appearance**, even though they are weaker than some other joints.

---

## Mitre Square vs Other Angle Tools
| Tool | Angle Provided | Main Use | Accuracy |
|----|---------------|----------|----------|
| Mitre Square | 45° | Mitre joints | Very high |
| Try Square | 90° | Square joints | Very high |
| Set Square | 45°, 60°, 90° | Layout work | High |
| Sliding Bevel | Any angle | Adjustable angles | Medium–High |

A mitre square is the **most accurate tool for fixed 45° marking**.

---

## Mitre Square vs Set Square
- **Mitre square**
  - Designed specifically for 45°
  - More robust for workshop use
  - Better for checking mitre cuts

- **Set square**
  - Lighter and more versatile
  - Mainly used for drawing and layout
  - Less suitable for checking sawn cuts

---

## Best Practice When Using a Mitre Square
- Always work from a **face side or face edge**
- Hold the stock firmly against the edge
- Use a sharp pencil or marking knife
- Do not let the tool slip during marking
- Check the cut after sawing
- Store carefully to avoid bending the blade

Accuracy of mitre joints depends on **careful marking and checking**.

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Keep fingers clear when marking with sharp tools
- Do not force the square against uneven edges
- Store safely to avoid damage
- Check edges for sharp corners

Mitre squares are safe when used correctly.

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
A mitre square is suitable when:
- Accurate 45° angles are required
- Mitre joints are being used
- Visual quality is important
- Frames or corners must align neatly

It is less suitable when:
- 90° angles are required (use a try square)
- Adjustable angles are needed (use a sliding bevel)

## Overall Summary
A mitre square is a **specialist marking out and checking tool** used to **mark and verify accurate 45° angles**, primarily for **mitre joints in woodworking**. With a **stock and blade fixed at 45°**, it allows precise layout and checking of angled cuts used in **frames, mouldings, and decorative products**. Compared to a set square, the mitre square offers **greater robustness and accuracy for workshop use**. In A Level Product Design, the mitre square should be understood as an **essential angle‑marking tool** that supports **high‑quality joint construction, accurate assembly, and professional finish**.
