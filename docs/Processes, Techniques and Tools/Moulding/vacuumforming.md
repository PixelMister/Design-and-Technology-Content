# Vacuum Forming

## What is Vacuum Forming?
Vacuum forming is a **plastic forming (moulding) process** used to shape **heated thermoplastic sheets** over a mould using **vacuum pressure**. Air is removed from between the sheet and the mould, forcing the plastic to take the mould’s shape.

In Product Design, vacuum forming is commonly used for **packaging, trays, housings, and prototypes**, especially in **low to medium volume production**.

---

## How Vacuum Forming Works
1. A **thermoplastic sheet** is clamped into a frame
2. The sheet is heated until soft and flexible
3. The mould is raised (or the sheet is lowered) into position
4. A **vacuum pump removes air** from beneath the sheet
5. Atmospheric pressure forces the plastic over the mould
6. The plastic cools and hardens
7. The formed sheet is released
8. Excess material is trimmed away

The shape of the product is determined by the **mould**.

---

## Key Components of a Vacuum Forming Machine

### Heater
- Softens the plastic sheet
- Usually infrared heaters
- Must heat evenly to prevent thinning

### Clamp Frame
- Holds the plastic sheet in place
- Prevents movement during forming

### Mould
- Can be made from:
  - Wood
  - MDF
  - Resin
  - Aluminium
- Defines the final shape

### Vacuum Pump
- Removes air from beneath the sheet
- Creates pressure to pull plastic over the mould

---

## Types of Moulds Used

### Male (Positive) Mould
- Plastic is formed **over** the mould
- Better detail on the outside surface
- Common in school workshops

### Female (Negative) Mould
- Plastic is pulled **into** the mould
- Better detail on the inside surface
- More industrial use

---

## Materials Suitable for Vacuum Forming
Vacuum forming uses **thermoplastics**, including:

- **High Impact Polystyrene (HIPS)**
- **PVC**
- **Acrylic (PMMA)**
- **PET**
- **ABS**

Materials must:
- Soften when heated
- Retain shape when cooled

---

## Advantages of Vacuum Forming
- Low tooling cost
- Simple moulds
- Fast setup
- Suitable for small batch production
- Good surface finish
- Ideal for prototyping
- Can be done in school workshops

---

## Disadvantages of Vacuum Forming
- Limited detail compared to injection moulding
- Uneven wall thickness
- Excess waste from trimming
- Lower strength than moulded plastics
- Not suitable for very high production volumes
- Limited shape complexity (no undercuts)

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Vacuum forming is commonly used for:

- **Blister packaging**
- **Food trays**
- **Protective packaging**
- **Display packaging**
- **Product housings**
- **Point‑of‑sale displays**
- **Prototypes and models**

It is especially popular in **packaging design**.

---

## Vacuum Forming vs Injection Moulding
| Feature | Vacuum Forming | Injection Moulding |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| Tooling Cost | Low | Very high |
| Production Volume | Low–Medium | Very high |
| Detail | Medium | Very high |
| Unit Cost | Medium | Low at scale |
| Waste | High (trim) | Low |

---

## Design Considerations for Vacuum Forming
Good vacuum‑formed designs include:
- **Draft angles** to allow release
- **Rounded corners** to avoid thinning
- **Even wall thickness**
- **Vent holes** (industrial)
- Avoiding undercuts

Designing for manufacture is essential.

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Low energy tooling
- Suitable for recycled plastics
- Lightweight products
- Ideal for short runs (less waste than mould making)

### Disadvantages
- High material waste from trimming
- Plastics may be difficult to recycle
- Single‑use packaging issues

### Improvements
- Recycled plastic sheets
- Improved trimming efficiency
- Better product design to reduce waste

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Hot plastic sheets
- Risk of burns
- Moving machinery
- PPE required:
  - Heat‑resistant gloves
  - Eye protection
- Safe for school workshops with supervision

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Vacuum forming is suitable when:
- Low tooling cost is required
- Prototypes or small batches are needed
- Packaging products are designed
- School workshop manufacture is required

Vacuum forming is not suitable when:
- Very high accuracy is required
- Complex internal features are needed
- Mass production is required
- Very thick or solid products are needed

## Overall Summary
Vacuum forming is a **plastic forming process** that shapes **heated thermoplastic sheets** over a mould using **vacuum pressure**. It is widely used for **packaging, trays, housings, and prototypes**, offering **low tooling cost, fast setup, and suitability for small to medium production runs**. However, vacuum forming has limitations such as **uneven wall thickness, limited detail, and material waste from trimming**, making it less suitable for mass production. In A Level Product Design, vacuum forming should be evaluated by considering its **process, materials, advantages and disadvantages, sustainability, design limitations, and comparison with injection moulding and blow moulding**.
