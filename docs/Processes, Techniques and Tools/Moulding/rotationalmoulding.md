# Rotational Moulding

## What is Rotational Moulding?
Rotational moulding (often shortened to **rotomoulding**) is a **plastic moulding manufacturing process** used to produce **hollow plastic products** by heating and rotating a mould containing plastic powder.

Unlike blow moulding, rotational moulding **does not use pressure**. Instead, the plastic coats the inside of the mould evenly as it rotates.

In Product Design, rotational moulding is used for **large, hollow, durable products** with **uniform wall thickness**.

---

## How Rotational Moulding Works
1. A measured amount of **thermoplastic powder** is placed into a mould
2. The mould is sealed shut
3. The mould is heated in an oven
4. The mould **rotates on two axes** (bi‑axial rotation)
5. The plastic melts and coats the inside of the mould evenly
6. The mould continues rotating while cooling
7. The plastic solidifies
8. The mould is opened
9. The hollow product is removed

The shape of the product is defined by the **inside of the mould**.

---

## Key Components of a Rotational Moulding System

### Mould
- Made from **aluminium or steel**
- Two or more parts
- Reusable
- Defines the shape and surface finish

---

### Heating Oven
- Heats the mould evenly
- Allows plastic powder to melt and flow

---

### Rotating Arm
- Rotates the mould on **two axes**
- Ensures even coating
- Prevents thin or weak areas

---

### Cooling Station
- Uses air or water spray
- Allows plastic to solidify evenly

---

## Materials Suitable for Rotational Moulding
Rotational moulding uses **thermoplastics in powder form**, including:

- **Polyethylene (PE)**
  - Low‑density (LDPE)
  - Medium‑density (MDPE)
  - High‑density (HDPE)
- **Polypropylene (PP)** (less common)
- **PVC** (limited use)

Polyethylene is the most common due to its:
- Toughness
- Impact resistance
- Ease of moulding

| Tooling Cost | Medium |
| Unit Cost | Medium |
| Surface Finish | Good |
| Internal Stress | Very low |

---

## Advantages of Rotational Moulding
- Uniform wall thickness
- No internal stresses
- Thick corners and edges
- No high pressure required
- Strong, impact‑resistant products
- Low waste (no runners or sprues)
- Moulds cheaper than injection moulding
- Inserts and threads can be moulded in

---

## Disadvantages of Rotational Moulding
- Slower production cycle
- Limited material choice
- Less fine detail than injection moulding
- Higher unit cost than blow moulding
- Not suitable for very thin walls
- Not ideal for mass production

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Rotational moulding is commonly used to manufacture:

- **Water tanks**
- **Fuel tanks**
- **Storage containers**
- **Road barriers**
- **Playground equipment**
- **Kayaks and canoes**
- **Bins and recycling containers**
- **Large hollow furniture**

It is ideal for **large, durable plastic products**.

---

## Rotational Moulding vs Blow Moulding
| Feature | Rotational Moulding | Blow Moulding |
|-------|--------------------|--------------|
| Pressure | None | Compressed air |
| Wall Thickness | Uniform | Can be uneven |
| Tooling Cost | Medium | High |
| Production Speed | Slow | Very fast |
| Typical Products | Tanks, bins | Bottles |

---

## Rotational Moulding vs Injection Moulding
| Feature | Rotational Moulding | Injection Moulding |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Product Type | Hollow | Solid |
| Tooling Cost | Medium | Very high |
| Production Volume | Low–Medium | Very high |
| Detail | Medium | Very high |
| Internal Stress | Very low | Higher |

---

## Design Considerations for Rotational Moulding
Good designs for rotational moulding include:
- Uniform wall thickness
- Rounded corners
- Avoiding sharp internal angles
- Even material distribution
- Moulded‑in features (threads, inserts)

No need for draft angles as parts are not forced out under pressure.

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Very little material waste
- Excess plastic powder can be reused
- Strong products with long lifespan
- Suitable for recycled plastics

### Disadvantages
- Energy‑intensive heating process
- Uses petrochemical plastics
- Slower cycle time increases energy per part

### Improvements
- Recycled polyethylene
- Energy‑efficient ovens
- Lightweight product design

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- High temperatures
- Rotating machinery
- Heavy moulds
- PPE required in industry:
  - Heat‑resistant gloves
  - Eye protection
  - Protective footwear

Rotational moulding is an **industrial process** and **not suitable for school workshops**.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Rotational moulding is suitable when:
- Large hollow products are required
- Uniform wall thickness is important
- Impact resistance is needed
- Low internal stress is essential
- Medium production volumes are acceptable

Rotational moulding is not suitable when:
- Very high production volumes are needed
- Fine detail is required
- Thin‑walled products are needed
- Low unit cost is critical

## Overall Summary
Rotational moulding is a **plastic moulding process** used to create **large, hollow products** by heating and rotating a mould containing **thermoplastic powder**. The rotation ensures **even material distribution**, resulting in **uniform wall thickness and low internal stress**, making products **strong and impact resistant**. Although rotational moulding has **slower cycle times and limited material choice**, it offers **lower tooling costs than injection moulding** and minimal material waste. In A Level Product Design, rotational moulding should be evaluated in terms of its **process, materials, advantages and disadvantages, sustainability, and suitability for producing durable hollow products**.
