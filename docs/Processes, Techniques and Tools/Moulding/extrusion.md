# Extrusion

## What is Extrusion?
Extrusion is a **continuous manufacturing process** used to create products with a **constant cross‑section** by forcing material through a **shaped die**.

In Product Design, extrusion is most commonly associated with **plastics, metals, and food products**, and is widely used for **long components such as pipes, tubes, frames, and profiles**.

---

## How Extrusion Works
1. Raw material (plastic granules or metal billet) is heated
2. The softened or molten material is pushed through a **die**
3. The material takes the **shape of the die opening**
4. The extruded product emerges continuously
5. The product is cooled
6. It is cut to the required length

The cross‑section of the product remains **the same along its entire length**.

---

## Key Components of an Extrusion System

### Hopper (Plastics)
- Holds plastic granules
- Feeds material into the heated barrel

---

### Heated Barrel
- Softens or melts the material
- Maintains controlled temperature

---

### Screw or Ram
- Pushes material forward
- Generates pressure for extrusion

---

### Die
- Shaped opening
- Determines the final cross‑section
- Made from hardened steel

---

### Cooling System
- Air or water cooling
- Solidifies the material after extrusion

---

### Cutter
- Cuts extruded material to length
- Produces uniform components

---

## Types of Extrusion

### Plastic Extrusion
- Uses thermoplastics
- Material is melted and pushed through a die

Used for:
- Pipes
- Tubing
- Window frames
- Cable insulation

---

### Metal Extrusion
- Uses heated metal billets
- Very high pressure required

Used for:
- Aluminium profiles
- Structural components
- Frames

---

### Hot Extrusion
- Material is heated
- Easier to deform
- Lower force required

---

### Cold Extrusion
- Material extruded at room temperature
- Higher strength and surface finish
- Requires more force

## Key Properties of Extrusion
| Feature | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Product Type | Constant cross‑section |
| Production Volume | Medium–Very high |
| Tooling Cost | Medium |
| Unit Cost | Low at scale |
| Accuracy | High |
| Material Waste | Low |

---

## Advantages of Extrusion
- Continuous production
- Very little material waste
- High production efficiency
- Consistent cross‑section
- Suitable for long components
- Can produce complex profiles
- Good surface finish (especially plastics)

---

## Disadvantages of Extrusion
- Limited to uniform cross‑sections
- High initial die cost
- Design changes are expensive
- Not suitable for complex 3D shapes
- Limited material choices compared to moulding

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Extrusion is used to manufacture:

- **Plastic pipes and tubing**
- **Window frames**
- **Cable insulation**
- **Plastic channels**
- **Aluminium frames**
- **Heat sinks**
- **Curtain rails**
- **Structural profiles**

Many construction and household products are extruded.

---

## Extrusion vs Injection Moulding
| Feature | Extrusion | Injection Moulding |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Product Shape | Constant cross‑section | Complex 3D |
| Production | Continuous | Cyclic |
| Tooling Cost | Medium | Very high |
| Waste | Very low | Low |
| Typical Products | Pipes, profiles | Casings |

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Minimal waste
- Excess material can be recycled
- Energy‑efficient for long runs
- Lightweight products

### Disadvantages
- Uses thermoplastics (often petrochemical‑based)
- Energy used for heating
- Recycling depends on material purity

### Improvements
- Recycled plastics
- Bio‑based polymers
- Lightweight extrusion designs

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- High temperatures
- Moving machinery
- Risk of burns
- PPE required in industry:
  - Heat‑resistant gloves
  - Eye protection
- Industrial guarding systems required

Extrusion is an **industrial process**, not suitable for school workshops.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Extrusion is suitable when:
- Long products are required
- Cross‑section is consistent
- High production volumes are needed
- Low material waste is important

Extrusion is not suitable when:
- Complex 3D shapes are needed
- Small batch production is required
- Frequent design changes are expected

## Overall Summary
Extrusion is a **continuous manufacturing process** used to produce **long products with a constant cross‑section** by forcing material through a **shaped die**. It is widely used with **plastics and metals**, particularly for products such as **pipes, frames, channels, and profiles**, offering **low material waste, high efficiency, and low unit cost at scale**. However, extrusion is limited to uniform cross‑sections and requires **expensive tooling**, making it unsuitable for complex 3D shapes or small production runs. In A Level Product Design, extrusion should be evaluated in terms of its **process, materials, production scale, sustainability, and comparison with other moulding and forming methods**.
