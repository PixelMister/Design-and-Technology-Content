# Blow Moulding

## What is Blow Moulding?
Blow moulding is a **plastic moulding manufacturing process** used to produce **hollow plastic products** by inflating heated plastic inside a mould using **compressed air**.

In Product Design, blow moulding is most commonly associated with the production of **bottles, containers, and hollow packaging**, especially in **mass production**.

---

## How Blow Moulding Works
1. Plastic granules are melted and formed into a **hot tube of plastic** called a *parison*
2. The parison is placed inside a **two‑part mould**
3. The mould closes around the parison
4. **Compressed air** is blown into the parison
5. The plastic expands and presses against the mould walls
6. The plastic cools and solidifies
7. The mould opens
8. The finished hollow product is removed
9. Excess plastic is trimmed

The shape of the product is defined by the **inside of the mould**.

---

## Key Components of a Blow Moulding System

### Parison
- Tube of hot, soft plastic
- Expands when air is blown inside
- Thickness affects final wall thickness

### Mould
- Usually made from **steel or aluminium**
- Two halves that close around the parison
- Can be reused thousands of times

### Air Supply
- Compressed air inflates the plastic
- Forces material against mould surface

---

## Types of Blow Moulding

### Extrusion Blow Moulding
- Parison is **extruded continuously**
- Mould closes around it
- Excess plastic is trimmed

Used for:
- Milk bottles
- Detergent bottles
- Fuel tanks

---

### Injection Blow Moulding
- Parison is **injection moulded first**
- Transferred to blow mould
- Very accurate neck finish

Used for:
- Pharmaceutical bottles
- Small containers

---

### Injection Stretch Blow Moulding (ISBM)
- Parison is stretched and blown
- Produces strong, lightweight products

Used for:
- PET drinks bottles

---

## Materials Suitable for Blow Moulding
Common thermoplastics include:

- **Polyethylene (HDPE, LDPE)**
- **Polypropylene (PP)**
- **Polyethylene terephthalate (PET)**
- **PVC** (less common)

Materials must be:
- Thermoplastic
- Capable of softening and reforming when heated

| Feature | Description |
|-------|-------------|

| Tooling Cost | High |
| Unit Cost | Low at scale |
| Accuracy | Medium–High |
| Surface Finish | Good |

---

## Advantages of Blow Moulding
- Ideal for hollow products
- High production speed
- Low unit cost for mass production
- Thin, lightweight products
- Smooth internal and external surfaces
- Minimal waste (excess often recycled)
- Consistent quality

---

## Disadvantages of Blow Moulding
- High initial tooling cost
- Limited shape complexity
- Wall thickness can be uneven
- Not suitable for solid products
- Limited material choice
- Design changes are expensive

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Blow moulding is commonly used to produce:

- **Drinks bottles**
- **Milk bottles**
- **Detergent containers**
- **Fuel containers**
- **Cosmetic packaging**
- **Medical containers**
- **Plastic drums**

Almost all plastic bottles are blow moulded.

---

## Blow Moulding vs Injection Moulding
| Feature | Blow Moulding | Injection Moulding |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| Product Type | Hollow | Solid |
| Typical Products | Bottles | Casings |
| Production Volume | Very high | Very high |
| Tooling Cost | High | Very high |
| Accuracy | Medium–High | Very high |

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Lightweight products reduce transport emissions
- Excess plastic can be recycled
- Efficient material use

### Disadvantages
- Uses petrochemical plastics
- Energy‑intensive process
- Difficult‑to‑recycle composite packaging

### Improvements
- Recycled plastics (e.g. rPET bottles)
- Bio‑based plastics
- Lightweighting designs
- Improved recycling systems

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- High temperatures
- Compressed air systems
- Moving machinery
- Industrial guarding required
- PPE used in industry:
  - Heat‑resistant gloves
  - Eye protection

Blow moulding is an **industrial process**, not suitable for school workshops.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Blow moulding is suitable when:
- Hollow plastic products are required
- High production volumes are needed
- Lightweight packaging is important
- Unit cost must be low at scale

Blow moulding is not suitable when:
- Solid components are needed
- Small batch production is required
- Complex internal features are required
- Frequent design changes are expected

## Overall Summary
Blow moulding is a **plastic moulding process** used to manufacture **hollow products** by inflating heated thermoplastic inside a mould using **compressed air**. It is widely used for producing **bottles and containers** in very high volumes, offering **low unit cost, lightweight products, and consistent quality**. However, blow moulding involves **high tooling costs, limited design flexibility, and reliance on thermoplastics**, making it unsuitable for small production runs or solid components. In A Level Product Design, blow moulding should be evaluated in terms of its **process, material suitability, production scale, sustainability, and comparison with other plastic moulding methods**.
