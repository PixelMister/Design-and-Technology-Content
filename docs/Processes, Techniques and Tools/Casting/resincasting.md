# Resin Casting

## What is Resin Casting?
Resin casting is a **manufacturing process** used to create components by **pouring liquid resin into a mould**, where it **cures (hardens)** to form a solid object. It is commonly used for **prototyping, small‑batch production, and model making**.

In A Level Product Design, resin casting is often used to produce **high‑quality prototypes and detailed components** without the cost of industrial tooling.

---

## Why Resin Casting is Used
Resin casting is used because it:
- Produces **detailed and accurate parts**
- Has **low tooling costs**
- Is ideal for **small batch production**
- Allows fast design changes
- Can replicate complex shapes
- Gives a good surface finish

It is especially useful where **injection moulding would be too expensive**.

---

## How Resin Casting Works
Resin casting uses a **reusable mould**, often made from silicone rubber.

### Step‑by‑Step Process
1. A **master pattern** (original model) is created
2. The pattern is placed into a mould box
3. **Liquid silicone rubber** is poured over the pattern
4. The silicone cures to form a flexible mould
5. The master pattern is removed
6. **Liquid resin** is mixed (resin + hardener)
7. Resin is poured into the mould
8. The resin cures (sets)
9. The mould is opened
10. The finished casting is removed and trimmed

The silicone mould can be reused **multiple times**.

---

## Types of Resins Used
Common resins used in resin casting include:

### Polyester Resin
- Low cost
- Strong smell
- Common in workshops
- Used with glass fibre

### Epoxy Resin
- Higher quality
- Low shrinkage
- Excellent surface finish
- Strong and durable
- More expensive

### Polyurethane Resin
- Fast curing
- Wide range of properties
- Often used for prototyping
- Can simulate plastics like ABS

---

## The Mould
Resin casting typically uses **silicone rubber moulds** because they:
- Are flexible
- Capture fine detail
- Release castings easily
- Can be reused many times

However, silicone moulds:
- Wear out over time
- Are not suitable for very high temperatures

## Advantages of Resin Casting
- Low start‑up cost
- Excellent surface detail
- Good dimensional accuracy
- Fast turnaround
- Suitable for complex shapes
- Ideal for prototyping
- Reusable moulds

---

## Disadvantages of Resin Casting
- Not suitable for mass production
- Limited mould lifespan
- Slower than injection moulding
- Health and safety risks
- Resins can be brittle
- Manual process

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Resin casting is commonly used for:

- **Prototypes**
- **Product models**
- **Small batch production**
- **Casing and enclosures**
- **Jewellery**
- **Automotive and design prototypes**
- **Replacement parts**

It is widely used in **design development stages**.

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Low material waste
- Reusable moulds
- Reduces need for expensive tooling
- Suitable for recycled fillers

### Disadvantages
- Resins are usually petrochemical‑based
- Difficult to recycle cured resin
- Hazardous chemicals involved

### Improvements
- Bio‑based resins
- Improved ventilation and safety
- Designing for longer product life

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Resins and hardeners can be toxic
- Fumes may be harmful
- PPE required:
  - Gloves
  - Goggles
  - Aprons
- Good ventilation essential
- Care needed with mixing ratios
- Suitable for supervised school workshops

---

## Suitability for Product Design
Resin casting is suitable when:
- Prototypes are required
- Small batch production is needed
- Complex shapes are required
- High surface detail is important
- Low tooling cost is essential

Resin casting is not suitable when:
- Mass production is required
- Very high strength is needed
- High temperature resistance is required
- Fast cycle times are essential

## Overall Summary
Resin casting is a **low‑cost, flexible manufacturing process** that involves pouring **liquid resin into reusable silicone moulds** to create solid components. It is ideal for **prototyping and small batch production**, offering **high surface quality, good accuracy, and design flexibility** without the high tooling costs of injection moulding. However, resin casting is **labour‑intensive, unsuitable for mass production, and involves health and safety risks** due to chemical resins. In A Level Product Design, resin casting should be evaluated in terms of its **process, materials, advantages and disadvantages, sustainability, and suitability for prototyping and limited production runs**.
