# Die Casting

## What is Die Casting?
Die casting is a **high‑pressure metal casting process** where **molten metal is injected into a reusable steel mould (die)** at high speed and pressure. The metal cools and solidifies quickly, producing **highly accurate components with a smooth surface finish**.

Die casting is mainly used for **mass production of metal parts**, especially from **non‑ferrous metals**.

---

## Why Die Casting is Used
Die casting is used because it:
- Produces **very accurate components**
- Gives an **excellent surface finish**
- Is suitable for **high‑volume production**
- Produces complex shapes with thin walls
- Requires little or no machining

It is widely used in **automotive, electronics, and consumer products**.

---

## How Die Casting Works
Die casting uses a **permanent metal mould**.

### Step‑by‑Step Process
1. The steel **die (mould)** is closed and clamped
2. Molten metal is **injected under high pressure** into the die cavity
3. The metal fills the mould very quickly
4. The metal cools and solidifies
5. The die opens
6. **Ejector pins** push the casting out
7. Excess material (sprues and flash) is removed
8. The die is closed ready for the next cycle

This cycle is **very fast**, making die casting ideal for mass production.

---

## The Die (Mould)
- Made from **hardened steel**
- Can be reused **thousands or millions of times**
- Designed with:
  - Cooling channels
  - Ejector pins
  - Gates and runners

Dies are **very expensive to produce**, but cost‑effective over large quantities.

---

## Metals Suitable for Die Casting
Die casting is mainly used with **low‑melting‑point, non‑ferrous metals**, including:

- **Aluminium alloys**
- **Zinc alloys**
- **Magnesium alloys**
- **Lead alloys** (less common)

Ferrous metals (steel, cast iron) are **not suitable** due to high melting temperatures.

---

## Types of Die Casting

### Hot Chamber Die Casting
- Metal is melted inside the machine
- Faster cycle times
- Used for:
  - Zinc
  - Magnesium

### Cold Chamber Die Casting
- Molten metal is poured into the chamber
- Slower than hot chamber
- Used for:
  - Aluminium
  - Copper alloys

|-------|-------------|
| Tooling Cost | Very high |
| Production Volume | Very high |
| Surface Finish | Excellent |
| Dimensional Accuracy | Very high |
| Production Speed | Very fast |
| Post‑Machining | Minimal |

---

## Advantages of Die Casting
- Excellent surface finish
- High dimensional accuracy
- Suitable for thin‑walled components
- Fast production rates
- Low unit cost at high volumes
- Consistent quality
- Little finishing required

---

## Disadvantages of Die Casting
- Very high tooling cost
- Only suitable for non‑ferrous metals
- Not economical for small batch production
- Limited part size (compared to sand casting)
- Design changes are expensive

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Die casting is commonly used for:

- **Car engine components**
- **Gear housings**
- **Electronic casings**
- **Tool bodies**
- **Camera and phone housings**
- **Appliance components**
- **Door handles and fittings**

It is ideal for **complex, high‑volume metal products**.

---

## Die Casting vs Other Casting Methods

### Die Casting vs Sand Casting
| Feature | Die Casting | Sand Casting |
|-------|------------|--------------|
| Mould Type | Permanent steel die | Single‑use sand mould |
| Accuracy | Very high | Medium |
| Surface Finish | Smooth | Rough |
| Tooling Cost | Very high | Low |
| Production Speed | Very fast | Slow |
| Volume | High | Low–Medium |

### Die Casting vs Investment Casting
| Feature | Die Casting | Investment Casting |
|-------|------------|-------------------|
| Accuracy | Very high | Very high |
| Surface Finish | Excellent | Excellent |
| Production Speed | Very fast | Slow |
| Best for | Mass production | Precision, low volume |
| Mould Reuse | Yes | No |

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Very little waste per component
- Reusable dies
- Suitable for recycled aluminium and zinc
- Minimal machining reduces waste

### Disadvantages
- Energy‑intensive process
- High embodied energy in steel dies
- Limited to specific metals

### Improvements
- Increased use of recycled metals
- Energy‑efficient furnaces
- Lightweight component design

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
- Extremely high temperatures
- High‑pressure injection systems
- Risk of burns and molten metal splash
- Heavy machinery
- PPE required:
  - Heat‑resistant gloves
  - Face shields
  - Protective clothing
- Industrial process only
- Not suitable for school workshops

---

## Suitability for Product Design
Die casting is suitable when:
- Very high production volumes are required
- Excellent surface finish is important
- Tight tolerances are needed
- Thin‑walled, complex components are required
- Unit cost must be low at scale

Die casting is not suitable when:
- Small batch production is required
- Low tooling cost is essential
- Ferrous metals are needed
- Large components are required

## Overall Summary
Die casting is a **high‑pressure metal casting process** that uses **reusable steel dies** to produce **highly accurate components with excellent surface finish**. It is best suited to **mass production of non‑ferrous metal parts**, such as aluminium and zinc components used in automotive, electronic, and consumer products. Although die casting has a **very high tooling cost**, it offers **fast production speeds, low unit cost at scale, and consistent quality**. In A Level Product Design, die casting should be evaluated in terms of its **accuracy, surface finish, production volume, cost, sustainability, and suitability for high‑volume manufacturing**.
