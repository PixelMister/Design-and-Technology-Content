# Milling and Routing

## What Are Milling and Routing?
Milling and routing are **subtractive manufacturing processes** where **rotating cutting tools** remove material to shape a workpiece. They are used to produce **accurate shapes, slots, holes, edges, and surface finishes**.

- **Milling** is usually associated with **metals and rigid materials**
- **Routing** is commonly used for **timber, plastics, and sheet materials**

In Product Design, milling and routing are important for **precision manufacturing, CAM processes, and batch production**.

---

## Milling vs Routing
| Feature | Milling | Routing |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Materials | Metals, plastics | Wood, plastics, MDF |
| Machine Type | Milling machine | Router / CNC router |
| Accuracy | Very high | High |
| Speed | Slower | Faster |
| Typical Use | Engineering parts | Furniture, panels |

---

## Specialist Tools Used in Milling

### Milling Machines
Milling machines are the **primary specialist tool**.

Types include:
- **Vertical milling machine**
- **Horizontal milling machine**
- **CNC milling machine**

Purpose:
- Precisely remove material
- Produce slots, pockets, profiles, and holes
- Suitable for metals and engineering plastics

---

### Milling Cutters
Rotating tools that remove material.

Common types:
- **End mills**
- **Slot drills**
- **Face mills**
- **Ball‑nose cutters**
- **Side and face cutters**

Purpose:
- Shape the workpiece
- Create flat surfaces, curves, or slots

Different cutters are chosen depending on **material and shape**.

---

### Work‑Holding Tools (Milling)
Secure the workpiece during machining.

- **Machine vice**
- **Clamps**
- **T‑slot bolts**
- **Fixtures and jigs**

Purpose:
- Prevent movement
- Improve accuracy
- Ensure safety

---

## Specialist Tools Used in Routing

### Routers
Routers spin cutting tools at high speed.

Types include:
- **Hand‑held router**
- **Table‑mounted router**
- **CNC router**

Purpose:
- Cut profiles, grooves, rebates, and joints
- Shape edges and surfaces
- Used mainly for wood and plastics

---

### Router Bits (Cutters)
Different bits are used for different operations.

Common types:
- **Straight bit**
- **Profile bit**
- **Chamfer bit**
- **Round‑over bit**
- **Flush trim bit**
- **V‑groove bit**

Purpose:
- Shape edges
- Cut joints
- Produce decorative details

---

### Guides and Fences
Used to control accuracy.

- **Edge guides**
- **Router fences**
- **Templates**
- **Bearing‑guided bits**

Purpose:
- Maintain straight cuts
- Improve repeatability
- Increase safety

---

## CNC Milling and CNC Routing Tools

### CNC Machines
Computer‑controlled milling or routing machines.

Purpose:
- High accuracy
- Repeatable results
- Batch and mass production

Used with:
- CAD/CAM software
- Tool paths
- Automated cutting cycles

---

### Tool Changers
- Automatically change cutters
- Increase efficiency
- Reduce setup time

---

## Measuring and Quality Control Tools
Used to check accuracy and finish.

- **Vernier calipers**
- **Steel rule**
- **Micrometer**
- **Depth gauge**
- **Surface gauge**

Purpose:
- Ensure dimensional accuracy
- Maintain tolerances
- Quality assurance

---

## Finishing Tools (Post‑Machining)
After milling or routing, parts may need finishing.

- **Files**
- **Sandpaper**
- **Deburring tools**
- **Abrasive wheels**

Purpose:
- Remove sharp edges
- Improve surface finish
- Prepare for assembly or finishing

---

## Health and Safety Equipment (Essential Specialist Tools)

### Mandatory PPE
- Safety goggles
- Ear protection
- Dust mask / respirator
- Protective footwear
- Aprons or overalls

### Safety Systems
- Emergency stop buttons
- Machine guards
- Dust extraction systems

Milling and routing can produce **high noise, dust, and flying debris**.

## Advantages of Milling and Routing
- High accuracy
- Excellent surface finish
- Suitable for complex shapes
- Repeatable results
- Compatible with CAD/CAM
- Wide range of materials

---

## Disadvantages of Milling and Routing
- Expensive equipment
- Requires skilled setup
- Tool wear
- Noise and dust
- Slower than moulding for mass production
- Not suitable for very soft materials

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Milling and routing are used for:

- **Furniture components**
- **Panels and casings**
- **Metal brackets and parts**
- **Prototypes**
- **Mould making**
- **Signs and lettering**
- **Precision engineering components**

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Milling and routing are suitable when:
- High accuracy is required
- CAD/CAM is used
- Batch production is needed
- Prototypes or precision parts are produced

They are less suitable when:
- Very large volumes are required
- Complex hollow forms are needed
- Low cost per unit is essential

---

## Exam Tips (A Level)
- Distinguish clearly between **milling and routing**
- Name **specific tools** (end mill, router bit, vice)
- Link tools to **materials**
- Mention **CNC for CAM**
- Include **health and safety**
- Compare with casting or moulding where relevant

## Overall Summary
Milling and routing are **subtractive manufacturing processes** that use **rotating cutting tools** to shape materials with high accuracy. Milling is typically used for **metals and engineering plastics**, while routing is mainly used for **timber, MDF, and plastics**. Specialist tools such as **milling machines, routers, CNC systems, cutters, work‑holding devices, and safety equipment** allow designers and manufacturers to produce **precise, repeatable components**. In A Level Product Design, milling and routing should be evaluated in terms of their **accuracy, suitability for CAD/CAM, production scale, safety requirements, and material compatibility**.
