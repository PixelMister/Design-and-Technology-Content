# Drilling

## What is Drilling?
Drilling is a **subtractive manufacturing process** used to create **cylindrical holes** in a material using a **rotating cutting tool called a drill bit**. Material is removed as the drill bit cuts into the workpiece.

In Product Design, drilling is one of the **most common and essential manufacturing processes**, used across **wood, metal, plastic, and composite materials**.

---

## How Drilling Works
1. The workpiece is secured in position
2. A drill bit rotates at speed
3. The drill bit is fed into the material
4. Cutting edges remove material
5. Swarf (waste material) is expelled
6. A hole of a specific diameter and depth is produced

Accuracy depends on:
- Drill bit type
- Machine used
- Work‑holding method
- Operator skill

## Types of Drilling Machines

### Hand Drill
- Portable
- Used for light work
- Less accurate

Used for:
- DIY
- Assembly
- On‑site work

---

### Pillar Drill (Bench or Floor Drill)
- Fixed vertical drilling machine
- High accuracy
- Adjustable speed

Used for:
- Metalwork
- Woodwork
- Plastics
- Repetitive drilling

Most common **school and workshop drilling machine**.

---

### Radial Drill
- Large industrial drill
- Adjustable arm
- Used for large components

Used in:
- Engineering workshops
- Heavy industry

---

### CNC Drilling Machine
- Computer‑controlled
- Extremely accurate
- Repeatable results

Used in:
- Batch production
- Mass production
- CAM manufacturing

## Specialist Drilling Tools

### Drill Bits
Rotating cutting tools used to make holes.

Common types:
- **Twist drill bit** – general purpose
- **Brad point bit** – wood
- **Spade bit** – large holes in wood
- **Forstner bit** – flat‑bottom holes
- **Masonry bit** – concrete and brick
- **Centre drill** – starting accurate holes
- **Countersink bit** – chamfered holes

Drill bits are chosen based on:
- Material
- Hole size
- Required finish

---

### Chucks
- Hold the drill bit in place
- Tightened using:
  - Chuck key
  - Keyless mechanism

---

### Work‑Holding Tools
Secure the workpiece safely.

- **Machine vice**
- **Clamps**
- **V‑blocks** (round stock)
- **Drill jigs**

Purpose:
- Prevent movement
- Improve accuracy
- Increase safety

---

### Drill Jigs
- Guide the drill bit
- Ensure repeatability
- Used in batch production

---

## Measuring and Accuracy Tools
Used to ensure holes are correct.

- **Vernier calipers**
- **Steel rule**
- **Depth gauge**
- **Go/No‑Go gauges**

Purpose:
- Check hole diameter
- Check hole depth
- Maintain tolerances

---

## Finishing Tools (Post‑Drilling)

- **Deburring tool**
- **Countersink**
- **Files**
- **Sandpaper**

Purpose:
- Remove sharp edges
- Improve safety
- Prepare holes for fasteners

---

## Health and Safety Equipment (Essential)

### Mandatory PPE
- Safety goggles
- Ear protection (if required)
- Protective footwear
- Apron or overalls

### Safety Practices
- Secure workpiece (never hold by hand)
- Remove chuck key before starting
- Keep long hair tied back
- Do not wear loose clothing
- Use correct speed for material
- Clear swarf using a brush, not hands

Drilling is suitable for **school workshops** when safety rules are followed.

## Advantages of Drilling
- Simple and versatile process
- Low equipment cost
- Suitable for many materials
- Accurate with correct setup
- Essential for assembly and fixing
- Easy to automate (CNC)

---

## Disadvantages of Drilling
- Limited to round holes
- Can cause tear‑out in wood
- Tool wear
- Generates swarf and heat
- Requires correct speed selection
- Risk of inaccuracy if poorly clamped

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Drilling is used for:
- **Bolt and screw holes**
- **Dowel joints**
- **Ventilation holes**
- **Assembly preparation**
- **Fixtures and fittings**
- **Prototype manufacture**
- **Engineering components**

Almost all manufactured products involve drilling at some stage.

---

## Drilling Compared to Other Processes
| Process | Function | Accuracy |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Drilling | Round holes | High |
| Milling | Slots, pockets | Very high |
| Routing | Profiles, grooves | High |
| Laser cutting | Profiles | Very high |
| Punching | Holes in sheet | Medium–High |

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Drilling is suitable when:
- Holes are required for fixings
- Accuracy is important
- Prototypes or batch production are made
- School workshop manufacture is needed

Drilling is less suitable when:
- Complex shapes are required
- Non‑circular holes are needed
- Very high production speed is required

## Overall Summary
Drilling is a **fundamental subtractive manufacturing process** used to produce **accurate cylindrical holes** in a wide range of materials. Using specialist tools such as **pillar drills, drill bits, chucks, vices, and jigs**, drilling can achieve **high accuracy, repeatability, and safety** in both school and industrial settings. Although limited to round holes, drilling is essential for **assembly, fixing, and structural design**. In A Level Product Design, drilling should be evaluated in terms of its **accuracy, material suitability, safety requirements, production scale, and role alongside other machining processes such as milling and routing**.
