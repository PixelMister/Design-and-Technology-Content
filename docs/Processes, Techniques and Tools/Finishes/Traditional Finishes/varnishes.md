# 3.5 Finishes – Varnishes

## What Are Varnishes?
Varnishes are **transparent surface finishes** applied mainly to **wood** to **protect the surface while allowing the natural grain to remain visible**. Unlike paint, varnish does **not contain pigment**.

In A Level Product Design, varnishes are evaluated in terms of:
- Protection
- Appearance
- Durability
- Application method
- Health and safety
- Sustainability

---

## Functions of Varnish Finishes
Varnishes are used to:
- Protect wood from:
  - Moisture
  - Dirt
  - Wear and abrasion
  - UV damage
- Enhance the **natural appearance of wood**
- Seal porous surfaces
- Improve durability and lifespan
- Provide different surface finishes (matt, satin, gloss)

---

## Main Components of Varnish
Varnishes contain similar components to paint, but **without pigment**:

### Resin
- Forms the protective film
- Determines hardness and durability

### Solvent
- Keeps varnish liquid during application
- Evaporates as varnish dries

### Additives
- Improve flow
- Control drying time
- Increase UV or moisture resistance

---

## Types of Varnish (A Level Focus)

## Oil‑Based Varnish
A **traditional solvent‑based varnish**.

### Advantages
- Very durable
- Excellent moisture resistance
- Deep, rich finish
- Enhances wood grain

### Disadvantages
- Long drying time
- Strong fumes
- High VOCs
- Yellowing over time

### Typical Uses
- Furniture
- Floors
- Doors
- Outdoor woodwork (exterior grades)

---

## Water‑Based Varnish
A **modern, water‑based alternative**.

### Advantages
- Low odour
- Quick drying
- Low VOCs
- Non‑yellowing
- Easy clean‑up

### Disadvantages
- Less durable than oil‑based
- Can raise wood grain
- Less traditional appearance

### Typical Uses
- Interior furniture
- School projects
- Cabinets
- Display items

✅ Safest varnish for **school workshops**.

---

## Polyurethane Varnish
A **high‑performance varnish** (oil‑ or water‑based).

### Advantages
- Extremely durable
- Scratch‑resistant
- Good chemical resistance
- Available in matt, satin, gloss

### Disadvantages
- Difficult to repair
- Can look artificial
- Solvent‑based versions have strong fumes

### Typical Uses
- Wooden floors
- Table tops
- High‑wear furniture

---

## Yacht (Marine) Varnish
A **flexible, weather‑resistant varnish**.

### Advantages
- Excellent UV resistance
- Flexible (prevents cracking)
- Highly water resistant

### Disadvantages
- Expensive
- Long drying time
- Strong fumes

### Typical Uses
- Outdoor furniture
- Boats
- Garden products

---

## Shellac (Traditional Varnish)
A **natural resin varnish** dissolved in alcohol.

### Advantages
- Quick drying
- Natural appearance
- Easy to repair
- Traditional finish

### Disadvantages
- Poor water resistance
- Low durability
- Not suitable for heavy use

### Typical Uses
- Antique furniture
- Musical instruments
- Decorative woodwork

---

## Varnish Finishes (Appearance)
Varnishes are available in different finishes:

| Finish | Appearance |
|------|------------|
| Matt | Low reflection, natural look |
| Satin | Soft sheen |
| Gloss | High shine, highlights grain |

Finish choice affects **aesthetics and perceived quality**.

---

## Surface Preparation for Varnishing
Good preparation is essential:

- Sand progressively (fine grit)
- Remove dust
- Apply sanding sealer (optional)
- Apply thin coats
- Lightly sand between coats

Poor preparation leads to:
- Rough finish
- Visible brush marks
- Trapped dust

---

## Application Methods
Varnish can be applied using:
- Brush
- Roller (limited)
- Spray (industrial)

Thin, even coats produce the **best finish**.

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
### Risks
- Solvent fumes
- Flammability
- Skin irritation

### Safety Measures
- Ventilated workspace
- PPE (gloves, mask)
- No naked flames
- Safe storage of solvents

✅ Water‑based varnishes are safest for education.

---

## Sustainability and Environmental Impact
### Advantages
- Extends product lifespan
- Reduces need for replacement
- Some water‑based options are low‑VOC

### Disadvantages
- Solvent‑based varnishes release VOCs
- Difficult to recycle varnished wood

### Improvements
- Use water‑based varnishes
- Apply minimal coats
- Design products for long life

---

## Varnish vs Paint
| Feature | Varnish | Paint |
|------|--------|-------|
| Transparency | Transparent | Opaque |
| Shows grain | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Durability | Medium–High | Medium–High |
| Colour | Natural | Any |
| Repair | Easier | Harder |

Varnish is chosen to **show material quality**, paint to **change appearance**.

---

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Varnishes are suitable when:
- Wood grain should be visible
- Protection is required
- Furniture or interior products are designed
- A natural finish is desired

Varnishes are less suitable when:
- Colour change is required
- Extreme durability is needed (use laminate or coating)
- Outdoor exposure without specialist varnish

## Overall Summary
Varnishes are **transparent surface finishes** used mainly on **wood** to provide **protection while enhancing the natural grain and appearance**. Different types of varnish—such as **oil‑based, water‑based, polyurethane, yacht varnish, and shellac**—offer varying levels of **durability, appearance, drying time, and environmental impact**. While varnishes provide effective protection and aesthetic quality, they rely heavily on **good surface preparation** and raise **health and sustainability considerations**, particularly with solvent‑based products. In **A Level Product Design**, varnishes should be evaluated in terms of **material suitability, performance, application method, safety, and environmental responsibility**.
