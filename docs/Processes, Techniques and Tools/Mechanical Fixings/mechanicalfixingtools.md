# Mechanical Fixings – Specialist Tools

## What Are Specialist Tools for Mechanical Fixings?
Specialist tools for mechanical fixings are **tools and equipment used to accurately install, tighten, align, and secure mechanical joints** such as **screws, bolts, nuts, washers, rivets, and press‑fit components**.

In A Level Product Design, understanding these tools helps you:
- Explain **how joints are made accurately**
- Justify **manufacturing and assembly methods**
- Discuss **safety, quality, and efficiency**
- Compare **manual and industrial processes**

---

## Why Specialist Tools Are Important
Specialist tools are used to:
- Ensure correct tightening force
- Prevent damage to materials
- Improve accuracy and repeatability
- Increase production speed
- Improve safety
- Maintain consistent quality in batch production

Incorrect tools can cause:
- Stripped threads
- Loose joints
- Cracked materials
- Joint failure

---

## Specialist Tools for Screws

### Screwdrivers
Used to drive screws into materials.

Common types:
- **Flat‑head (slotted)**
- **Phillips**
- **Pozidriv**
- **Torx**
- **Hex (Allen key)**

**Purpose:**
- Apply rotational force
- Match screw head to prevent cam‑out (slipping)

---

### Powered Screwdrivers / Drill Drivers
- Battery or mains powered
- Often torque‑controlled

**Purpose:**
- Faster assembly
- Consistent tightening
- Used in batch and mass production

---

## Specialist Tools for Nuts and Bolts

### Spanners
Used to tighten or loosen nuts and bolt heads.

Types:
- **Open‑ended spanner**
- **Ring spanner**
- **Combination spanner**
- **Adjustable spanner**

**Purpose:**
- Apply controlled torque
- Prevent rounding of nuts

---

### Socket Sets
- Used with ratchets
- Fit over nuts and bolt heads

**Purpose:**
- Faster tightening
- Better grip
- Common in engineering and automotive manufacture

---

### Torque Wrench (Industrial)
- Applies a **specific tightening torque**

**Purpose:**
- Prevent overtightening
- Ensure safety‑critical joints
- Used in professional manufacture

---

## Specialist Tools for Washers
Washers do not require separate tools but are used with:
- Screwdrivers
- Spanners
- Sockets

Correct washer selection is part of **specialist joint preparation**, not tool use.

---

## Specialist Tools for Rivets

### Rivet Gun (Pop Rivet Gun)
- Manual or powered

**Purpose:**
- Pulls mandrel
- Expands rivet body
- Creates permanent joint

Used for:
- Pop (blind) rivets
- One‑sided access joints

---

### Pneumatic / Hydraulic Riveters (Industrial)
**Purpose:**
- High‑speed riveting
- Consistent force
- Used in mass production

---

## Specialist Tools for Press Fixings

### Arbor Press / Mechanical Press
- Applies controlled vertical force

**Purpose:**
- Install press‑fit components
- Bearings
- Shafts
- Bushes

---

### Vice
- Common in school workshops

**Purpose:**
- Controlled pressing
- Light‑duty press fits
- Accurate alignment

---

### Jigs and Guides
- Hold components in correct alignment

**Purpose:**
- Prevent damage
- Ensure accurate press fitting
- Improve repeatability

---

## Drilling and Preparation Tools
Mechanical fixings often require **preparation**.

### Pillar Drill / Hand Drill
- Used to drill:
  - Pilot holes
  - Clearance holes

**Purpose:**
- Prevent splitting
- Improve accuracy
- Ensure correct fit

---

### Taps and Dies (Metalwork)
- **Tap**: cuts internal threads
- **Die**: cuts external threads

**Purpose:**
- Create threads for bolts and machine screws

---

## Measuring and Inspection Tools
Used to ensure fixings are correct.

- **Vernier callipers** – hole and bolt size
- **Micrometer** – shaft diameter
- **Go / No‑Go gauges** – tolerance checking

These tools ensure **quality control**.

---

## Health and Safety Equipment (Essential)

### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- Safety goggles
- Gloves (where appropriate)
- Protective footwear

---

### Safety Practices
- Correct tool selection
- Secure workpieces
- Avoid overtightening
- Keep hands clear of moving tools
- Use guards on powered tools

Mechanical fixing tools can cause **injury if misused**.

## Advantages of Specialist Mechanical Fixing Tools
- Accurate joints
- Improved safety
- Faster production
- Consistent results
- Reduced material damage
- Suitable for batch and mass production

---

## Disadvantages of Specialist Tools
- Cost of equipment
- Training required
- Setup time
- Industrial tools not available in schools
- Maintenance required

## Overall Summary
Specialist tools for mechanical fixings are essential for **accurate, safe, and reliable assembly** of products using **screws, nuts, bolts, washers, rivets, and press‑fit joints**. Tools such as **screwdrivers, spanners, socket sets, rivet guns, presses, and drilling equipment** allow manufacturers to control **alignment, tightening force, and joint quality**, particularly in **batch and mass production**. While many industrial tools are not used directly in school workshops, understanding their function is vital in **A Level Product Design** for analysing joining methods, justifying manufacturing choices, and addressing **quality, safety, sustainability, and efficiency**.
