# Polystyrene Cement

## What Is Polystyrene Cement?
Polystyrene cement is a **specialist solvent‑based adhesive** designed specifically for **bonding polystyrene plastic**. Rather than acting like a conventional glue, it works by **softening and partially dissolving the polystyrene surfaces**, which then **fuse together** as the solvent evaporates.

In A Level Product Design, polystyrene cement is commonly used for:
- **Model making**
- **Prototyping**
- **Small plastic components**
- **Educational and hobby work**

---

## How Polystyrene Cement Works
Polystyrene cement works through **solvent welding**.

### Step‑by‑Step Process
1. Polystyrene cement is applied to one or both surfaces
2. The solvent **softens the polystyrene**
3. The parts are pressed together
4. The softened plastic merges at the joint
5. The solvent evaporates
6. A **strong, rigid, permanent bond** is formed

✅ The joint becomes almost as strong as the original material.

---

## Key Characteristics of Polystyrene Cement
- Solvent‑based adhesive
- Designed only for polystyrene
- Creates a **rigid, permanent bond**
- Low gap‑filling ability
- Slower setting than cyanoacrylate
- Strong once fully cured
- Chemical bond rather than surface bond

---

## Materials Suitable for Polystyrene Cement
Polystyrene cement is suitable for bonding:

- **Polystyrene (HIPS, GPPS)** ✅
- Thin polystyrene sheet
- Polystyrene model components

⚠️ It works **only** when both materials are polystyrene.

---

## Materials Not Suitable for Polystyrene Cement
Polystyrene cement is **not suitable** for:
- Acrylic (PMMA)
- PVC
- Polypropylene
- Polyethylene
- Metal
- Wood
- Mixed‑material joints

Using it on the wrong material will result in **weak or failed joints**.

---

## Types of Polystyrene Cement

### Liquid Polystyrene Cement
- Thin, water‑like consistency
- Applied with a brush or needle applicator
- Drawn into joints by **capillary action**

**Used for:**
- Tight‑fitting joints
- Small model components
- Neat, accurate assembly

---

### Thick / Gel Polystyrene Cement
- Thicker consistency
- Slightly better gap filling

**Used for:**
- Less precise joints
- Larger model parts
- Beginner model making

---

## Advantages of Polystyrene Cement
- Strong bond for polystyrene
- Neat joint with no visible glue line
- Creates a true chemical weld
- Ideal for small, precise components
- Low cost
- Easy to apply in small amounts

---

## Disadvantages of Polystyrene Cement
- Only works on polystyrene
- Strong solvent fumes
- Not repositionable once joined
- Slow full curing time
- Poor gap‑filling ability
- Brittle joint under impact

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
Polystyrene cement is commonly used for:

- **Scale models**
- **Architectural models**
- **Product prototypes**
- **Educational kits**
- **Small plastic assemblies**
- **Display models**

It is widely used during **design development**, not final manufacture.

## Polystyrene Cement vs Other Adhesives
| Adhesive | Suitable for Polystyrene | Bond Type | Typical Use |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------|-------------|
| Polystyrene cement | ✅ Yes | Solvent weld | Models |
| Cyanoacrylate | ✅ Yes | Surface bond | Quick fixes |
| Acrylic cement | ❌ No | Solvent weld | Acrylic |
| Epoxy resin | ⚠️ Limited | Structural bond | Mixed materials |
| Hot‑melt glue | ⚠️ Limited | Thermoplastic | Prototypes |

Polystyrene cement is the **best choice for polystyrene‑to‑polystyrene joints**.

---

## Application Methods
Polystyrene cement is applied using:
- Fine brush
- Needle applicator
- Precision nozzle

Best practice:
- Ensure parts fit accurately
- Apply sparingly
- Hold parts firmly together
- Allow sufficient curing time
- Avoid excess adhesive

---

## Health and Safety Considerations
Polystyrene cement contains **volatile solvents**.

### Hazards
- Strong fumes
- Flammable
- Skin and eye irritation

### Safety Precautions
- Use in a **well‑ventilated area**
- Avoid naked flames
- Wear eye protection
- Avoid skin contact
- Keep lid closed when not in use
- Follow manufacturer’s instructions

In school workshops, use is **supervised and controlled**.

---

## Environmental Considerations
### Disadvantages
- Solvent‑based
- VOC emissions
- Difficult to recycle solvent‑welded plastics

### Improvements
- Use minimal quantities
- Design to reduce joints
- Use alternative joining methods where appropriate

## Suitability for A Level Product Design
Polystyrene cement is suitable when:
- Polystyrene components are being joined
- Neat, accurate joints are required
- Model making or prototyping is needed

- Materials are mixed
- Structural strength is required
- Flexibility is needed

## Overall Summary
Polystyrene cement is a **specialist solvent‑based adhesive** used exclusively for **bonding polystyrene plastic**. It works by **softening and fusing the material surfaces together**, forming a **strong, rigid, and permanent joint** once the solvent evaporates. It is particularly well suited to **model making and prototyping**, where **neat, accurate joints** are required. However, its use is limited to polystyrene only, and it involves **health and safety risks due to solvent fumes**. In A Level Product Design, polystyrene cement should be evaluated as a **material‑specific adhesive**, ideal for **development‑stage plastic models**, but unsuitable for mixed‑material or structural applications.
