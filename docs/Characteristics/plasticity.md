# Plasticity

## What is Plasticity?
Plasticity is a **material property** that describes a material’s ability to **deform permanently without breaking when a force is applied**. Once the force is removed, the material **does not return to its original shape**.

In Product Design, plasticity is important for **forming, shaping, and manufacturing processes**, such as bending, pressing, rolling, and moulding.

---

## Plastic vs Elastic Deformation
When a force is applied to a material, it can behave in two main ways:

### Elastic Deformation
- Temporary change in shape
- Material returns to original shape when the force is removed
- Example:
  - Rubber band stretching

### Plastic Deformation
- Permanent change in shape
- Material does **not** return to its original shape
- Example:
  - Bent aluminium sheet
  - Pressed metal car panel

Plasticity refers **only to permanent deformation**.

---

## Structure and Composition
Plasticity depends on a material’s **internal structure and bonding**.

### Metals
- Layers of atoms can slide over each other
- Dislocations allow permanent deformation
- High plasticity in:
  - Aluminium
  - Copper
  - Mild steel

### Polymers
- Plasticity varies widely
- Thermoplastics:
  - High plasticity when heated
- Thermosets:
  - Very low plasticity (brittle)

### Ceramics
- Very low plasticity
- Strong bonds prevent atomic movement
- Tend to fracture rather than deform

### Composites
- Plasticity depends on matrix material
- Usually limited plastic deformation

---

## How Plasticity Works
- Force is applied to a material
- Material deforms elastically at first
- Once the **yield point** is reached:
  - Atomic layers slip
  - Permanent deformation occurs
- Further force increases plastic deformation until failure

The **yield point** marks the transition from elastic to plastic behaviour.

---

## Key Properties Related to Plasticity
| Property | Description |
|--------|------------|
| Plasticity | Ability to permanently deform |
| Yield Point | Stress at which plastic deformation begins |
| Ductility | Plastic deformation in tension |
| Malleability | Plastic deformation in compression |
| Toughness | Ability to absorb energy before failure |

---

## Advantages of High Plasticity
- Easy to shape and form
- Enables complex manufacturing processes
- Reduces risk of sudden fracture
- Useful for metal forming
- Allows permanent shaping without cracking

---

## Disadvantages of High Plasticity
- Loss of dimensional accuracy
- Risk of permanent damage in use
- Not suitable where shape must be maintained
- Can lead to creep over time
- Often lower stiffness

---

## Typical Uses in Product Design
### Materials with High Plasticity
- **Aluminium** – sheet metal forming, cans
- **Copper** – pipes, wiring
- **Mild steel** – car bodies, structural components
- **Thermoplastics** – injection moulded products

### Materials with Low Plasticity
- **Glass** – screens, containers
- **Ceramics** – tiles, insulators
- **Thermosetting plastics** – electrical components

---

## Plasticity and Manufacturing
Plasticity is essential in many manufacturing processes, including:
- **Rolling**
- **Forging**
- **Pressing**
- **Deep drawing**
- **Injection moulding**
- **Extrusion**

Without plasticity, many modern products could not be manufactured efficiently.

---

## Suitability for Product Design
High plasticity materials are suitable when:
- Permanent shaping is required
- Complex forms are needed
- Manufacturing efficiency is important
- Energy absorption is useful

Low plasticity materials are suitable when:
- Shape must be maintained
- High stiffness is required
- Brittle behaviour is acceptable
- Precision is critical

Good design balances **plasticity, strength, and elasticity**.

## Overall Summary
Plasticity is a key **material property** that describes a material’s ability to **deform permanently without breaking** once its elastic limit has been exceeded. Materials such as **metals and thermoplastics** show high plasticity, making them ideal for **forming and shaping processes** like rolling, pressing, and moulding. In contrast, materials such as **ceramics and glass** have very low plasticity and tend to fracture instead of deforming. In A Level Product Design, plasticity should be evaluated alongside **strength, elasticity, manufacturing method, and product function** to ensure materials are suitable for both production and use.
